P/N ratios are relative and cannot be compared across plates or studies. So in Custer et al. Custer et al. they run the same assay against a purified chicken IgY standard and report a concentration.

## 1. Standard curve and calculation

1. Prepare a dilution series of purified chicken IgY in coating buffer, coated in the same way and on the same plate as the samples. The concentration range needs to be established locally against the species working dilutions from Section 4 so that the samples fall on the linear portion of the curve rather than at either asymptote; Custer et al. do not publish their range.

2. Subtract the 630 nm absorbance from the 450 nm absorbance for every well. This is Custer et al.'s readout and it is worth taking even though Fassbinder-Orth et al. read 450 nm alone, because the 630 nm reference cancels well-to-well optical artefacts from scratches, dust and fingerprints at no cost in time or reagent.

3. Fit a four-parameter logistic curve to the standard series and interpolate sample concentrations from it.

4. Report as chicken IgY equivalents, per Section 1.

Keep the P/N calculation as well. It is the only number comparable to Fassbinder-Orth et al. and to the wider wild bird literature, and it costs nothing once the negative control is already on the plate.
